## “Express vs Impress”

Below are 10 points for employers to explore in a practical, reflective way. Each point includes a brief context, key information, 3 open-ended questions, and 3 freewriting prompts designed for use in training sessions.<!--more-->

---

## 1 - Clarity is more valuable than cleverness

- Context: In workplaces, people often confuse “sounding smart” with “communicating well.” Strong communication should help others understand, decide, and act.
- Information: When people are trying to impress, they may use jargon, long explanations, or status language. When they are trying to express, they focus on precision, structure, and relevance. Employers benefit from people who can make complex ideas simple.

---

### Open-ended questions:
- When have you noticed someone sounding impressive but not being clear?
- What words or phrases do you use when you want to sound competent rather than clear?
- How can clarity improve trust, speed, and accountability in your team?

---

### Freewriting exercises:

- Write about a time you misunderstood a manager, client, or colleague because the message was too polished and not clear enough.
- Freewrite for 5 minutes on the difference between being “understood” and being “admired.”
- Describe a message you wish you had communicated more simply at work.

---

## 2 - People remember connection, not performance

- Context: Communication is not just about what you say; it is about how people feel while hearing it. Employers often need a tone that invites cooperation rather than displays authority.
- Information: Impressing can create distance; expressing creates rapport. When leaders communicate with warmth, humility, and purpose, people listen with more openness and less defensiveness.

### Open-ended questions:

- What makes a message feel human rather than performative?
- In your experience, what has better long-term value: recognition or trust?
- How can managers communicate with power without creating fear?

---

### Freewriting exercises:

- Write about a conversation where someone made you feel seen and respected.
- Freewrite on one time you tried to sound authoritative and how it affected the room.
- Describe what “warm authority” sounds like in your own words.

---

## 3 - Authenticity builds credibility, not artificial polish

- Context: In a professional setting, people can become overly rehearsed. This often creates a gap between the person’s public image and their actual values or intentions.
- Information: The goal is not to become less professional; it is to become more real. Authentic communication is rooted in honesty, ownership, and self-awareness. It allows employees to build credibility over time.

---

### Open-ended questions:

- What do you think is lost when people perform professionalism instead of practicing it?
- How does authenticity affect trust in leadership?
- What part of your communication feels most natural, and what part feels forced?

---

### Freewriting exercises:

- Write about a work situation where you felt you had to hide your real opinion.
- Freewrite for 5 minutes on what being authentic would require in your role.
- Describe the difference between confidence and performance in your communication style.

---

## 4 - Listening is part of expressing well

- Context: Many people assume communication is about speaking well. In fact, skilled communication begins with listening closely enough to respond in a useful, respectful way.
- Information: People who focus on impressing often wait for their turn to speak. People who focus on expressing listen for needs, context, and emotional tone. Listening reduces misunderstanding and improves problem-solving.

---

### Open-ended questions:

- When you feel the urge to speak first, what are you really trying to achieve?
- How can better listening change team dynamics?
- What does a thoughtful response sound like in a high-pressure workplace conversation?

---

### Freewriting exercises:

- Write about a conversation where someone listened well and changed your perspective.
- Freewrite on a time you interrupted or rushed a conversation.
- Describe how your workplace would improve if everyone listened more before reacting.

---

## 5 - A useful message is better than a memorable one

- Context: People often equate “impactful communication” with dramatic language, strong opinions, or clever phrasing. However, leadership is most effective when a message helps people act wisely.
- Information: Expression serves purpose. It answers questions, resolves tension, clarifies intent, and helps teams move forward. Impressing may attract attention; expressing creates momentum.

---

### Open-ended questions:

- What makes a message truly useful to the listener?
- When has a message been memorable but not effective?
- How can your communication become more action-oriented?

---

### Freewriting exercises:

- Write a short message you would send to a team after a difficult project outcome.
- Freewrite on how you can make your next update more practical and less performative.
- Describe a decision you made at work and how a clearer message could have helped.

---

## 6 - Feedback should build people, not protect ego

- Context: Employers often give feedback in ways that either correct performance or defend status. The difference between expression and impression is visible in how feedback is framed.
- Information: Expressive communication tells the truth with respect. It focuses on behavior, impact, and growth. Impressive communication can become defensive, vague, or overly polished to avoid discomfort.

---

### Open-ended questions:

- What is the difference between giving feedback to win an argument and giving feedback to develop someone?
- How can managers make feedback feel safe rather than threatening?
- When have you received feedback that helped you grow—and what made it effective?

---

### Freewriting exercises:
- Write a feedback message you would like to receive from a manager.
- Freewrite on a time you avoided saying something honest because you feared looking harsh.
- Describe how you can give feedback with directness and dignity.

---

## 7 - Confidence does not require volume

- Context: Many employees associate confidence with speaking louder, faster, or more often. In reality, confident communication is often calm, grounded, and clear.
- Information: Impressing can become noisy. Expressing is confident without being dominant. Calm communication often signals maturity, steadiness, and emotional control—qualities highly valued by employers.

---

### Open-ended questions:

- What does confident communication look like in a room where everyone is speaking at once?
- How can a quieter person still command respect?
- What is the relationship between calmness and credibility?

---

### Freewriting exercises:
- Write about a workplace moment when someone’s calm tone changed the situation.
- Freewrite on the feeling of speaking up without needing to dominate the room.
- Describe a situation where you wish you had stayed composed instead of becoming reactive.

---

## 8 - Emotion should be managed, not hidden
- Context: People often think expressing emotions is unprofessional. But emotion is part of communication. The issue is not whether emotion exists, but whether it is acknowledged and handled well.
- Information: Impressing often tries to suppress vulnerability and emotion in order to appear controlled. Expressing allows emotional intelligence: naming what is happening, staying respectful, and choosing a constructive response.

---

### Open-ended questions:
- When is emotional honesty helpful in a workplace conversation?
- What happens when people hide emotion instead of channeling it?
- How do you know when you are reacting emotionally versus responding thoughtfully?

---

### Freewriting exercises:
- Write about a moment when you felt strongly at work and had to decide whether to speak up.
- Freewrite on what it means to be “emotionally professional.”
- Describe a difficult conversation where emotional awareness could have improved the outcome.

---

## 9 - Communication should adapt to the audience

- Context: The same message does not work equally well with every person or circumstance. Impressing often uses one style for everyone; expressing learns to adjust.
- Information: Skilled communicators adapt their tone, pace, examples, and level of detail to the audience. This requires empathy, social awareness, and the ability to ask the right questions before speaking at length.

---

### Open-ended questions:

- How do you change your message when speaking to senior leaders, peers, or junior staff?
- What happens when people use the same communication style with everyone?
- How can audience awareness improve both trust and influence?

---

### Freewriting exercises:

- Write about a conversation where the same message landed differently with two different audiences.
- Freewrite on how you adjust your communication when speaking to a skeptical audience.
- Describe the kind of listener you most struggle to reach and why.

---

## 10 - Long-term influence comes from integrity, not spotlight

- Context: The deepest form of professional communication is not about being impressive in the moment. It is about being remembered as someone who was clear, fair, and trustworthy over time.
- Information: In employer contexts, influence grows through consistency. When people express themselves sincerely and responsibly, others trust them more across repeated interactions. This is lasting influence, not momentary performance.

---

### Open-ended questions:

- What kind of professional reputation do you hope others build around you?
- Which matters more in the long run: being seen as polished or being seen as dependable?
- How can you communicate in a way that strengthens your character and your relationships?

---

### Freewriting exercises:

- Write a short personal statement on the kind of communicator you want to become.
- Freewrite on a time when your integrity affected the trust others placed in you.
- Describe the habits that would help you become more expressive and less performative over time.

---

## Reflection prompt for the whole session

- If your communication had to be judged on one question, would people say you express clearly or impress easily?
- What would need to change for them to say “express” instead?

This structure can be used as a workshop handout, slide deck outline, or facilitation guide for employer training.
